Pixle? What is that?

Pixle is a very short and casual game which challenges its players with different puzzles each day.
Puzzles are built from hand-made patterns and a handful of randomly chosen emojis. Our puzzle generator mixes things up and comes up with interesting and challenging puzzles, which are just waiting to be solved.
Games like Wordle and Picross inspired the development of Pixle. It combines different aspects of its spiritual predecessors and improves the overall gameplay, effectively making it a compact and exciting mixture. Besides playing on your own, you can challenge your friends and family to these nerve-racking puzzles!
Pixle is available on the web, and we challenge you to solve today's puzzle!

A short guide on how to play

Wondering how Pixle works? No worries, we will thoroughly explain.
Below you can see a grid, which we also refer to as a pixle. The pixle is a pattern consisting of up to five different emojis.

So, the grid represents a pixle. You are shown the solution of the pixle once at the beginning and as soon as it's hidden, you have to use your memory to rebuild it.
Doesn't seem too difficult, right?

If you place an emoji incorrectly, you lose a try on the mismatched tile. Don't worry, after each failed try, the pixle will flip and show the correct answers briefly.
However, as soon as you run out of available tries on any tile, it's game over!

About the puzzle creation process

Puzzles, or pixles as we like to call them, are a fundamental part of the game Pixle. After all, the name Pixle derives from them.
But more importantly: How are these pixles created?
Every pixle is generated fresh each day. The generator uses the date as its starting point, so everyone around the world gets the very same puzzle on the same day. It starts out with one of our hand-made patterns. These patterns are what make a pixle recognisable and memorable, instead of a random mess of emojis.
So that you don't learn them all by heart, the generator then mixes things up: it mirrors or rotates the pattern, combines two patterns of the same size into a new one and recolours some of its areas. Every result is checked, so a pixle never falls apart into scattered single tiles.
Finally, it fills the pattern with randomly chosen emojis and makes sure no emoji is used twice. After all, a pattern consisting of only red boxes wouldn't be as fun. A new pixle is born!
